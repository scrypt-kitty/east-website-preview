Project Raspberry

A proposed Google-linked (“Helios/Helio Capital”) hyperscale data-center campus at Greenfield, Botetourt County — advanced under a shell entity and NDAs. Active investigation

Overview

Public records indicate planning for 3.5 MGD at “Day 01” scaling toward 10–11 MGD at full build, a ~$170M dedicated water-main, and 120 diesel generators — against a public figure of “up to 8 MGD.” EAST analyzes the record and the gap between public framing and planning documents.

Issue Brief · EAST-2026-001 · v1.0 · Review status: Published

Botetourt, Roanoke region deserve real water numbers for Google's Project Raspberry

2026-07-09 · Author: Environmental Accountability & Strategy Team · Cite as: EAST (2026), Issue Brief EAST-2026-001.

📷 The water level is 14.7 ft below full pond at Carvins Cove Natural Reserve, July 9, 2026, Roanoke. (Photograph — SWVA DCTA)

The public has repeatedly heard that the project may need up to 8 million gallons of water per day. But public records show the planning discussions were larger. In July 2024, project questions asked how to reach 3.5 MGD for “Day 01” and discussed a dedicated water-main extension, tanks and pumps to achieve 10 to 11 MGD to the site. In April 2026, when WVWA asked whether Google wanted to keep a “Day 1 plus Day N” approach or “skip over Day 1,” a Google representative responded that the preferred approach was to proceed with Day N and skip Day 1.

(These documents were acquired through a Freedom of Information Act request.) That does not prove WVWA has agreed to provide 10 or 11 MGD — but it shows the public has not been given the full picture. Water capacity affects drought planning, reservoir reliability, infrastructure costs, ratepayer risk, and future users. If the larger scenario is no longer pursued, Google, WVWA and Botetourt County should say so in writing. If it remains, the public deserves to know before grading begins.

The question is simple: what is the true maximum water demand for Project Raspberry, who pays for the infrastructure, and what happens during drought? Those answers should be public. Not later. Now.
